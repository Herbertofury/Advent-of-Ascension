## v31: flame focus, disc channels and heel detailing

![Soul Storm v31 authoring render](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Herbertofury/Advent-of-Ascension/main/docs/soul-storm-v31-hero.png)

This combined authoring revision retains the baked grip and adds a broader side saddle, a more visible rooted flame, four tapered focused sight prongs with an open center, wider actual disc channels and symmetric oval heel settings. ### What the checks establish

- 101 sampled frames, 5,258,960 active triangle samples; declared inactive zero-scale projectiles are recorded separately.
- 132,660 scoped saddle/mechanical surface pairs with no reported intersections. This excludes plasma parts and does not prove containment or continuous-time clearance.
- Frame-zero mesh winding check found no negative-volume closed meshes. All five file-backed images are packed.
- The v25 leather maps and grip GLB were separately regenerated byte-for-byte from their published source archive; this does not certify the full v31 native material pipeline.

### Still below the final art bar

The emitter stack remains too boxy, the underside/rear too plain and the grip still reads rubber-like in some lighting. The circular core dominates the silhouette. These are open art defects, not waived QA gates. Actual arm fit, native animation/export, source projectile captures, multiplayer and measured performance remain unaccepted.

### v31 recoil transport: bounded tracks and matched glow ownership

Four position-only recoil clips now have synchronized solid and illuminated tracks. The paired tracks address a real integration risk: moving a solid emitter while its glowing geometry remains on a sibling track. The final native geometry must still provide the declared aliases or a verified owner-parent hierarchy.

The released TaCZ 1.1.8-hotfix2 animation parser accepted all **four clips / eight tracks / 328 keys** on Java 17 and 21 with documented helper stand-ins. Sampled linear approximation error stayed below 0.005 model units. Eight contract tests check malformed or inconsistent track data.

An original Lua presentation adapter reuses **four tracks**, keeps state per instance, ignores duplicate/out-of-order confirmed-event sequences, and attempts cleanup of all tracks even when one stop fails. Its 10,000-event mock-context fixture passed 20,022 assertions with one initial track allocation. There is no per-frame polling callback. This is a bounded-allocation design check, **not a Minecraft FPS result**.

The adapter does not consume ammunition, spawn projectiles or schedule gameplay. The actual confirmed-shot callback bridge, native bone binding, arms, effects, interruption and multiplayer remain open. Clip timing must not silently replace the source weapon’s fire interval.

## Reproduce and inspect

Open the selected `.blend`, or regenerate with Blender 4.3.2:

```sh
blender -b -t 4 --python build.py
blender -b -t 4 --python check.py
blender -b -t 4 --python render_motion.py
```

The check and motion scripts are provided for v9. The web animation samples fifteen Blender frames across half a second; it is an authoring preview, not gameplay footage or an approved rate-of-fire change.

The bounded QA sampled 121 times with no nonfinite emitter transforms, recoil-range violations or simultaneous recoil events. It found no zero-area polygons or duplicate-position vertices in the visible current mesh, and verified that the main flame's shape-key root stays fixed. The duplicated flame pole vertices found during the earlier iteration were welded before these checks.
